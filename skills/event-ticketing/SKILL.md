# Event Ticketing

Get an event selling, keep sales on track, and make the next one easier, with the user approving every public step.

## Ground rules
- Ask before publishing an event page, opening sales, announcing anything, or emailing attendees. Show exactly what will go out.
- Never create accounts or enter passwords, bank, or tax details. If a site needs a sign-in, ask the user to sign in themselves, then continue.
- Look up platform fees and features; they change. Say where the numbers came from.

## Steps
1. **Gather the basics:** event name, date and time, venue or online, capacity, audience, budget, and goal (revenue, filling the room, a fundraiser). Reuse details from a past event page or emails if there are any.
2. **Compare 2–3 ticketing options** that fit (a general ticketing platform, the venue's system, or a simple payment link): per-ticket fees, who pays them, payout timing, and features that matter (check-in, discount codes, waitlist, refunds).
3. **Plan tickets:** tiers (early bird, general, VIP, group), prices, quantity per tier, sales dates, discount codes, and the refund policy. Show revenue after fees at 50%, 75%, and 100% sold, and the break-even point.
4. **Write the event page:** title, a short hook, what attendees get, schedule, location and access, FAQs, and the refund policy.
5. **Set it up** on the chosen platform in the browser and stop before publishing. Add the event to the user's calendar.
6. **Plan the promotion:** a dated launch checklist plus drafts for each step — announcement posts for the user's social accounts, an email to their list, a reminder before early-bird pricing ends, and a last-call post. Each one goes out only on approval.
7. **Track sales.** Offer a scheduled task (weekly, then daily in the final week) that checks sales against the goal, and when they're behind, suggests a nudge: a reminder post, a limited discount code, or a group offer.
8. **Run the day:** draft attendee emails (confirmation details, a reminder 2 days before, day-of info with directions) and prepare a check-in list.
9. **Follow up:** draft a thank-you email with a short feedback survey, summarize attendance and revenue, and offer to set up the next event from this one.

## Output
A platform comparison, a ticket plan with revenue and break-even, the event page set up to publish, promotion and attendee drafts, sales tracking, and a post-event summary.
