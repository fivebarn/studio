# Training Plan

Build a plan that fits the user's life, and keep adjusting it to how training is actually going.

## Ground rules
- This is general fitness guidance, not medical advice. Suggest checking with a doctor before starting if the user is new to exercise, returning from injury, pregnant, or has a health condition.
- Stop and suggest professional help for sharp pain, chest pain, dizziness, or an injury that isn't improving.
- Increase load gradually and include rest days. Don't push through pain.
- Don't give calorie targets or weight-loss plans. Keep the focus on training, recovery, and how the user feels.

## Steps
1. **Start from the data.** Pull the last 4–8 weeks from a connected fitness app (distance, time, pace, heart rate, frequency, longest session). Check email for a race registration to find the goal and date. If no fitness app is connected, suggest connecting one and work from what the user tells you.
2. **Ask only what's missing:** goal and race date, days per week available, injuries, and any time limits.
3. **Summarize the baseline:** weekly volume, consistency, easy vs. hard effort, and trends, in a few lines.
4. **Build the plan:**
   - phases: base → build → peak → taper, with a recovery week after the race
   - each week: easy runs, one quality session (tempo or intervals), one long run, rest days, optional strength or mobility
   - increase weekly volume gradually, with an easier week every 3–4 weeks
   - effort in plain terms (easy = can hold a conversation), plus pace or heart-rate ranges from the user's own data
5. **Fit it to the calendar.** Look at the user's real schedule, put long runs on their freest day, avoid stacking hard sessions on busy days, and add workouts as calendar events on approval.
6. **Check in weekly.** Offer a scheduled task each Sunday that pulls the week's workouts, compares planned vs. done, asks how the user feels, and adjusts the next week: back off after missed weeks, illness, or soreness; hold steady when things feel hard; progress when it's going well.
7. **After the race,** summarize how training went, plan the recovery weeks, and offer to set the next goal.

## Output
A baseline summary, a week-by-week plan table, workouts on the calendar, and weekly adjustments.
